**Executive Summary**
This analysis evaluates the 7-year Total Cost of Ownership (TCO) for replacing 20 Class 8 tractors with battery-electric vehicles (BEVs) for regional routes in Indiana. Despite significant energy and maintenance savings, the high initial purchase price and infrastructure requirements result in a BEV TCO that is approximately $60,000 higher per truck than diesel over a 7-year period ($548,600 vs $488,538).

**Key Drivers**
1. **Capital Expenditure:** The BEV purchase price remains the primary barrier. Even with the $40,000 [unverified] IRA 45W credit, the net cost is double that of a diesel tractor.
2. **Energy Savings:** BEVs offer a massive operational advantage, reducing energy costs by approximately 59% ($245,538 - $100,800 = $144,738 savings per truck over 7 years).
3. **Maintenance:** Reduced moving parts in BEVs lead to an estimated 40% reduction in maintenance expenses ($0.09 [unverified]/mi vs $0.15 [unverified]/mi).
4. **Infrastructure:** The requirement for DC fast charging and grid upgrades adds a significant upfront cost of $50,000 [unverified] per vehicle.

**Recommendation**
Based on the current financial modeling, **I recommend continuing with Diesel tractors for the 2026 procurement cycle.** 

The BEV option does not yet achieve TCO parity under current Indiana energy rates and purchase prices. The transition to BEV would only be recommended if the company prioritizes carbon reduction goals over immediate cost optimization, or if additional state-level incentives are secured to offset the $60,022 TCO gap.
