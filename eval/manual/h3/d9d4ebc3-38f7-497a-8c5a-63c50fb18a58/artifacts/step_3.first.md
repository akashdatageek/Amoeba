### Memo: Recommendation on Fleet Electrification

**To:** Management
**From:** TCO Modeler
**Date:** October 11, 2026
**Subject:** TCO Analysis: Diesel vs. Battery-Electric Class 8 Tractors

**Recommendation**
It is recommended that the carrier **do not** replace the current diesel fleet with battery-electric tractors at this time. Based on the 7-year total cost of ownership (TCO) analysis, the battery-electric option is $169,200 ($643,200 - $474,000) more expensive per truck than the diesel alternative.

**Financial Logic**
While battery-electric tractors offer significant operational savings in fuel and maintenance, these are insufficient to offset the high initial capital requirements within a standard 7-year operational window.

1.  **Operational Savings:** BEVs reduce annual operating expenses (OpEx) from $42,000 ((60,000 / 7.0 * 3.85) + (60,000 * 0.15)) to $18,600 ((60,000 * 2.0 * 0.11) + (60,000 * 0.09)), resulting in an annual saving of $23,400 ($42,000 - $18,600) per truck.
2.  **Capital Premium:** The combined cost of the vehicle price premium (net of incentives) and the required charging infrastructure creates an upfront cost gap of $333,000 (($360,000 + $153,000) - $180,000) per truck.
3.  **Break-even Point:** At current energy rates and purchase prices, the break-even point for the BEV investment is approximately **14.2 years** ($333,000 / $23,400). This exceeds the 7-year analysis horizon and likely exceeds the useful life of the vehicle's battery system.

**Key Assumptions**
*   **Annual Mileage:** Constant at 60,000 miles per truck.
*   **Energy Pricing:** Diesel remains at $3.85/gal [unverified, Oct 2026] and commercial electricity remains at $0.11/kWh [unverified, Oct 2026] for the duration of the 7-year period.
*   **Infrastructure:** The total depot cost of $3,060,000 [S10, 2026] (Step 2) is allocated equally across 20 trucks.
*   **Residual Value:** No salvage or resale value was attributed to either vehicle type at the end of year 7.
