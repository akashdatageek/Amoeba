### Recommendation

**Recommendation: Do NOT replace the diesel fleet with battery-electric tractors at this time.**

**Logic:**
The financial analysis demonstrates that BEV tractors are not cost-competitive over the required 7-year horizon. Despite significant operational savings in energy and maintenance, the high upfront costs of the vehicles and the necessary charging infrastructure create a prohibitive financial barrier.

1.  **TCO Gap:** The total cost of ownership for a BEV tractor is $643,200, compared to $474,000 for a diesel tractor—a premium of $169,200 per vehicle ($643,200 - $474,000).
2.  **Operational Savings:** BEVs offer an annual operating expense (OpEx) saving of $23,400 per truck.
    *   Diesel Annual OpEx: (60,000 / 7.0 [S2] * $3.85 [unverified]) + (60,000 * $0.15 [unverified]) = $42,000
    *   BEV Annual OpEx: (60,000 * 2.0 [S2] * $0.11 [unverified]) + (60,000 * $0.09 [unverified]) = $18,600
    *   Annual Savings: $42,000 - $18,600 = $23,400
3.  **Break-even Point:** The upfront cost premium (Net Purchase + Infrastructure minus Diesel Purchase) is $333,000 [($360,000 + $153,000 [S10, S16]) - $180,000 [unverified]]. At current savings rates, it would take approximately **14.2 years** to break even on the investment ($333,000 / $23,400).

Because the break-even point far exceeds the 7-year analysis window and likely exceeds the operational lifespan of the tractor, the transition to BEVs is not financially justified under current Indiana market conditions and incentive structures.
