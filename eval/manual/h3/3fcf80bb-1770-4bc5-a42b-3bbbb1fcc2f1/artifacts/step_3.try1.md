### Recommendation

**MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Management
**FROM:** TCO Modeler
**DATE:** October 11, 2026
**SUBJECT:** Total Cost of Ownership Analysis: Diesel vs. Battery-Electric (BEV) Class 8 Tractors

**Executive Summary**
Based on a 7-year total cost of ownership (TCO) analysis, it is recommended that the carrier **do not** replace the current diesel fleet with battery-electric tractors at this time. The BEV option results in a total cost increase of **$166,200 per truck** ($640,200 - $474,000) over the 7-year horizon.

**Financial Analysis**
The transition to BEVs presents a significant conflict between high upfront capital expenditures and lower operational costs:

1.  **Upfront Cost Premium:** The combined cost of the BEV purchase price (after federal credits) and the necessary charging infrastructure creates an upfront premium of **$330,000** per truck compared to diesel (($360,000 + $150,000) - $180,000).
2.  **Operational Savings:** BEVs offer substantial efficiency gains. Annual energy and maintenance costs for a BEV are approximately **$18,600** ((60,000 * 2.0 * $0.11 [unverified]) + (60,000 * $0.09 [unverified])), compared to **$42,000** for diesel ((60,000 / 7.0 [S2] * $3.85 [unverified]) + (60,000 * $0.15 [unverified])), yielding an annual operational saving of **$23,400** per truck ($42,000 - $18,600).
3.  **Break-Even Point:** Due to the high initial investment, the break-even point for the BEV investment is approximately **14.1 years** ($330,000 / $23,400). This exceeds the 7-year analysis window and likely exceeds the useful operational life of the vehicle's battery system.

**Assumptions**
*   **Annual Mileage:** Constant at 60,000 miles per truck.
*   **Energy Pricing:** Diesel remains at $3.85/gal [unverified] and commercial electricity at $0.11/kWh [unverified] for the duration of the 7 years.
*   **Maintenance:** Costs per mile remain constant at $0.15 [unverified] for diesel and $0.09 [unverified] for BEV.
*   **Infrastructure:** The $150,000 [unverified] per-truck share of infrastructure is treated as a sunk cost at the start of the period.
*   **Residual Value:** No salvage value was assigned to either vehicle type at the end of year 7.

**Conclusion**
While BEVs are significantly cheaper to operate on a per-mile basis, the current purchase price and infrastructure requirements in Indiana make them financially unviable for this specific regional route profile over a 7-year period.
