**Executive Summary**
Based on a 7-year total cost of ownership analysis, it is recommended that the carrier **do not** replace the current diesel fleet with battery-electric vehicles (BEVs) at this time. The BEV option results in a total cost increase of **$198,764 per truck** ($678,831 - $480,067) over the 7-year horizon.

**Financial Analysis**
The transition to BEVs offers significant operational savings in energy and maintenance, reducing annual operating expenses by approximately **$23,034 per truck** compared to diesel, calculated as: (($237,067 + $63,000) - ($101,031 + $37,800)) / 7 = $23,034. However, these savings are insufficient to offset the high initial capital requirements:
*   **Purchase Price Premium:** The net purchase price of a BEV ($401,500) is $221,500 higher than a diesel tractor ($180,000), as the Federal Commercial Clean Vehicle Credit (45W) expired on September 30, 2025 [S45].
*   **Infrastructure Burden:** The required charging infrastructure adds a substantial $138,500 per truck to the initial investment [S10, S9, S94, S17].

**Break-Even Logic**
The total upfront premium for switching to BEV (Net Purchase Price + Infrastructure minus Diesel Purchase Price) is **$360,000 per truck** ($401,500 + $138,500 - $180,000). Given the annual operational savings of $23,034, the break-even point for the investment is approximately **15.6 years** ($360,000 / $23,034). This far exceeds the 7-year analysis window and the typical useful life of the tractor assets.
