### Memo: Recommendation

**To:** Management  
**From:** TCO Modeler  
**Date:** October 11, 2026  
**Subject:** Total Cost of Ownership Analysis: Diesel vs. Battery-Electric Class 8 Tractors

**Executive Summary**  
Based on a 7-year total cost of ownership (TCO) analysis for a fleet of 20 Class 8 tractors operating regional routes in Indiana, it is recommended that the carrier **continue using diesel tractors** rather than switching to battery-electric vehicles (BEVs) at this time. The BEV option results in a cost increase of **$166,200 per truck** (640,200 - 474,000) over the 7-year period.

**Financial Analysis**  
The analysis compared the costs of a standard diesel tractor against a BEV tractor, accounting for purchase prices, available incentives, energy costs, maintenance, and the necessary charging infrastructure.

*   **Upfront Costs:** The BEV requires a significantly higher initial investment. Even after applying the $40,000 Federal Commercial Clean Vehicle Credit (45W) [unverified], the net purchase price of a BEV ($360,000) and the associated infrastructure cost per truck ($150,000 [unverified]) create an upfront premium of **$330,000** ((360,000 + 150,000) - 180,000) over a diesel tractor.
*   **Operational Savings:** BEVs offer substantial operational advantages. Annual energy and maintenance costs for a BEV are approximately **$18,600** ((60,000 * 2.0 [S2] * 0.11 [unverified]) + (60,000 * 0.09 [unverified])), compared to **$42,000** ((60,000 / 7.0 [S2] * 3.85 [unverified]) + (60,000 * 0.15 [unverified])) for diesel, yielding an annual saving of **$23,400** (42,000 - 18,600) per truck.
*   **Break-Even Point:** Due to the high upfront premium, the operational savings take approximately **14.1 years** (330,000 / 23,400) to offset the initial investment. This exceeds the 7-year analysis horizon and likely exceeds the useful life of the vehicle's battery system.

**Assumptions**  
*   **Annual Mileage:** 60,000 miles per truck.
*   **Time Horizon:** 7 years.
*   **Energy Prices:** Indiana diesel at $3.85/gal [unverified] and commercial electricity at $0.11/kWh [unverified] remain constant.
*   **Efficiency:** Diesel efficiency of 7.0 MPG [S2] and BEV efficiency of 2.0 kWh/mile [S2].
*   **Maintenance:** Diesel at $0.15/mile [unverified] and BEV at $0.09/mile [unverified].
*   **Incentives:** Federal 45W credit of $40,000 [unverified] applied; Indiana state incentives are $0 [unverified].
*   **Residual Value:** No salvage or residual value was calculated for either vehicle type.

**Conclusion**  
While BEVs provide lower per-mile operating costs, the current purchase price premium and the high cost of charging infrastructure in Indiana make the transition financially unviable for this specific regional duty cycle. Unless federal or state incentives increase significantly or BEV purchase prices drop, diesel remains the most cost-effective choice.
