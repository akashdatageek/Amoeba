# Commercial Electricity Costs by State: 2026 Price Changes

Updated September 18, 2026 · EIA data through June 2026 · January–June and June-only comparisons with 2025 · Release: August 26, 2026 · Next scheduled EIA release: September 24

**Ohio, Virginia and Maryland had the largest percentage increases in commercial electricity prices in the first half of 2026 versus the same period of 2025.** EIA’s U.S. average rose from **13.06¢ to 13.86¢ per kWh, up 6.1%**. At an unchanged, labeled 3,000 kWh per month, that price difference represents **$24 more per month**. These are broad commercial-sector averages, not small-business tariffs or actual company bills.

## Where commercial electricity prices rose fastest: January–June

**44 of the 51 states/DC jurisdictions increased; seven decreased.** Ohio’s commercial average rose 24.5%, Virginia’s 22.8% and Maryland’s 21.3%. This compares January–June 2026 with January–June 2025, using EIA Table 5.6.B. The national 6.1% increase uses EIA’s U.S. figures, not an unweighted average of states.

**Highest price is a different question.** Hawaii had the highest first-half average at 41.98¢/kWh, compared with 36.86¢ in 2025. Its 5.12¢ increase would add $153.60 to a labeled 3,000-kWh month. Ohio’s larger percentage increase, from 11.07¢ to 13.78¢, would add $81.30 at that same usage. Neither is an observed small-business bill.

All retrieved September 18, 2026. Commercial columns only; 50 states plus DC, with U.S. Total separate. Census-division rows are excluded.

Percentage change = (2026 price − 2025 price) ÷ 2025 price × 100. Modeled dollar difference = assumed kWh × change in cents ÷ 100. Six-month scenarios hold the entered monthly usage constant for six months; they are not forecasts for the rest of 2026. All monetary changes are nominal. Published prices have two-decimal precision; calculations use those rounded source values. We use EIA’s year-to-date averages directly rather than averaging monthly or state prices.

Revenue-per-kWh averages can change with customer mix, consumption patterns and tariff components; these data alone do not isolate a utility rate increase or explain causes. No increase is attributed here to data centers, a particular law or summer weather. ## Three findings reporters can use

1. **Observed:** U.S. commercial retail electricity averaged **14.19¢/kWh** in June 2026, up **+0.65¢ (+4.80%)** from June 2025’s **13.54¢** (EIA EPM Table 5.6.A).
2. **Observed:** Among 50 states + DC, commercial ¢/kWh ranged from **North Dakota 8.02¢** to **Hawaii 48.05¢**. Top five: Hawaii 48.05¢, California 27.33¢, Massachusetts 24.52¢, New York 23.56¢, Alaska 23.42¢. Bottom five: North Dakota 8.02¢, Texas 8.66¢, Nebraska 8.82¢, Nevada 9.83¢, Oklahoma 9.9¢.
3. **Modeled (same kWh):** Holding kWh fixed at the documented bands, the same U.S. commercial average implies modeled monthly bills **$10–$23** higher than June 2025 prices — largest dollar gap on the higher-kWh daycare band. State ¢ differences dominate scenario bill spreads.

**Not a best-state ranking.** This page separates **observed EIA commercial ¢/kWh** from **documented kWh bands**. The average includes charges reflected in utility revenue; no customer-specific tariff is modeled. It is distinct from [pump fuel costs for mobile fleets](https://startbusinessbystate.com/fuel-costs-mobile-service-businesses/) (gasoline/diesel at the pump vs commercial power at the meter).

## Sources and methodology

**Observed prices:** EIA Electric Power Monthly Table **5.6.A**, commercial sector, June 2026 vs June 2025, 50 states + DC + U.S. Total. Workbook `table_5_06_a.xlsx`. Public EIA workbook.

**Hypothetical bills:** documented kWh/month × observed commercial ¢/kWh ÷ 100. See scenario CSV rationales. Never present as measured firm costs. The average includes charges reflected in utility revenue; no customer-specific tariff adjustment is modeled.

**Density:** BLS QCEW open annual CSVs, private ownership, 2025, industries 81211 / 23822 / 561720.

State commercial averages ≠ your utility rate schedule. Classification of commercial vs industrial customers varies by utility. Preliminary EIA-861M estimates may revise.

Frequently asked Are the monthly dollar bills real company costs?

No. They are **scenarios**: assumed kWh multiplied by observed EIA commercial averages. Replace the kWh with your meter data and your tariff for a real estimate — and still watch demand charges.

 Does the EIA average exclude demand charges?

No. EIA calculates the average from revenue divided by electricity sold; revenue includes energy, demand and service charges. The average does not specify your tariff’s separate components. Adding a generic demand fee on top could double-count costs. Use your own tariff and meter profile for an actual bill.

 How is this different from the fuel-costs page?

The [fuel-costs study](https://startbusinessbystate.com/fuel-costs-mobile-service-businesses/) uses EIA **pump** gasoline/diesel for mobile fleets. This page uses EIA **commercial retail electricity** ¢/kWh for shop meters.

 Is this a ranking of best states for service businesses?

No. Higher or lower commercial ¢ is one operating-cost input among many (wages, rent, licensing, demand). We do not score or rank “best” states here.
