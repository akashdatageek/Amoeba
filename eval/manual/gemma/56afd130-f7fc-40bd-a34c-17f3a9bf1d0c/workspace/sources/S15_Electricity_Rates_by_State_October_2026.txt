## About This Data

**Rates on this page reflect a blended average of utility-reported revenue per kilowatt-hour and live competitive market pricing.** For states with fully competitive retail markets, we supplement published utility data with real-time plan pricing from retail providers — the same data that powers our [city-level rate pages](https://www.electricchoice.com/electricity-prices-by-state/texas/) across 300+ markets.

Deregulated market claims are verified against state public utility commission filings, including the [Public Utility Commission of Texas (PUCT)](https://www.puc.texas.gov/), the [Public Utilities Commission of Ohio (PUCO)](https://puco.ohio.gov/), the [Pennsylvania PUC](https://www.puc.pa.gov/), the [Illinois Commerce Commission](https://www.icc.illinois.gov/), and the [New York Public Service Commission](https://www3.dps.ny.gov/). Rates are updated monthly; city-level competitive data refreshes four times daily. See our [Trust & Licensing](https://www.electricchoice.com/licenses-certifications/) page for editorial standards and regulatory credentials.

## FAQ

What is the average electricity rate in the US?

The average U.S. electricity rate is **18.34¢/kWh** for residential customers and **14.19¢/kWh** for commercial customers as of October 2026. Rates range from 13.11¢/kWh in [Nevada](https://www.electricchoice.com/electricity-prices-by-state/nevada/) to 52.72¢/kWh in [Hawaii](https://www.electricchoice.com/electricity-prices-by-state/hawaii/). The national average has risen 5.0% year-over-year. See the full [state-by-state rate table](https://www.electricchoice.com/electricity-prices-by-state#rates) above for every state’s current residential and commercial rates.

What state has the cheapest electricity?

**[Nevada](https://www.electricchoice.com/electricity-prices-by-state/nevada/)** has the cheapest residential electricity at 13.11¢/kWh, 29% below the national average. It is followed by [Nebraska](https://www.electricchoice.com/electricity-prices-by-state/nebraska/) (13.25¢), [Utah](https://www.electricchoice.com/electricity-prices-by-state/utah/) (13.37¢), [Louisiana](https://www.electricchoice.com/electricity-prices-by-state/louisiana/) (13.49¢), and [Tennessee](https://www.electricchoice.com/electricity-prices-by-state/tennessee/) (14.07¢). For commercial rates, [North Dakota](https://www.electricchoice.com/electricity-prices-by-state/north-dakota/) is cheapest at 8.02¢/kWh. Low rates in these states are driven by hydroelectric power, wind energy, abundant natural gas, and low population density. See the [top 5 cheapest states](https://www.electricchoice.com/electricity-prices-by-state#cheapest).

What state has the most expensive electricity?

**[Hawaii](https://www.electricchoice.com/electricity-prices-by-state/hawaii/)** has the most expensive electricity at 52.72¢/kWh, 187% above the national average. It imports petroleum for most power generation. [California](https://www.electricchoice.com/electricity-prices-by-state/california/) (34.74¢), [Massachusetts](https://www.electricchoice.com/electricity-prices-by-state/massachusetts/) (29.61¢), [Maine](https://www.electricchoice.com/electricity-prices-by-state/maine/) (29.59¢), and [New York](https://www.electricchoice.com/electricity-prices-by-state/new-york/) (29.49¢) round out the top five.

Six of the ten most expensive states are in New England. Aging grid infrastructure and reliance on imported natural gas drive costs higher across the region. See the [top 5 most expensive states](https://www.electricchoice.com/electricity-prices-by-state#most-expensive).

Why is my electric bill so high?

High electric bills are typically caused by above-average rates in your state, seasonal usage spikes (AC in summer, heating in winter), inefficient appliances, or being on your utility’s default rate instead of a competitive plan. In [deregulated states](https://www.electricchoice.com/map-deregulated-energy-markets/), switching providers can save 15–30%.

Check your cost per kWh on your bill and compare it to your state’s average in the [rate table above](https://www.electricchoice.com/electricity-prices-by-state#rates). See [average electric bills by state](https://www.electricchoice.com/average-electric-bills/) for a full cost breakdown. If you’re paying more, you may be overpaying.

Can I choose my electricity provider?

Yes, if you live in a deregulated state. **18 states plus D.C.** have some form of retail electricity choice: Texas, Pennsylvania, Ohio, Illinois, New York, New Jersey, Connecticut, Delaware, Maine, Maryland, Massachusetts, New Hampshire, Rhode Island, plus limited or capped markets in Michigan, Virginia, California, Oregon, and Nevada.

Customers in deregulated markets save 15–30% on average by [comparing plans](https://www.electricchoice.com/providers/). Your utility still delivers the power and handles outages. Only the generation company changes.

Why are electricity rates going up?

U.S. electricity rates have risen 5.0% year-over-year. Key drivers include:

*   Rising natural gas prices
*   Grid modernization and weather-hardening investments
*   Increased demand from data centers and electric vehicles
*   Renewable energy transition costs

[Hawaii](https://www.electricchoice.com/electricity-prices-by-state/hawaii/) (+28.7%), [Idaho](https://www.electricchoice.com/electricity-prices-by-state/idaho/) (+19.1%), and [Washington](https://www.electricchoice.com/electricity-prices-by-state/washington/) (+15.0%) saw the steepest increases. Connecticut (−10.6%) was the biggest decliner.
