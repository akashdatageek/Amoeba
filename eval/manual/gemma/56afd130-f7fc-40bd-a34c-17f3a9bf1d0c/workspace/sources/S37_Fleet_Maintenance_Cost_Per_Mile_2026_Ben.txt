Cost Per Mile Formula

## How to calculate fleet maintenance cost per mile

Fleet maintenance cost per mile is calculated by dividing total maintenance spend — parts, labor, outsourced repairs, tires, fluids, and compliance-related servicing — by the total miles driven in the same period. The formula is simple, but the data collection is where most fleets fail. Missing work orders, untracked parts, and lump-sum repair invoices distort the number and hide the real cost drivers.

Total Maintenance Cost ÷ Total Miles Driven = Cost Per Mile (CPM)

Parts Filters, belts, brakes, tires, fluids, DEF

Labor In-house technician hours + outsourced shop rates

Downtime Revenue lost while asset is out of service

Compliance DOT inspections, emissions, annuals, drug pool

## Fleet maintenance cost per mile: frequently asked questions

### What is a good fleet maintenance cost per mile in 2026?

A good fleet maintenance cost per mile in 2026 is $0.15–$0.18 for Class 8 over-the-road tractors and $0.12–$0.16 for lighter Class 3–5 vans. Top-quartile fleets hold total maintenance CPM — parts, labor, tires, and compliance — under $0.20 across mixed fleets. Anything above $0.28 for a Class 8 truck signals reactive maintenance patterns, aging assets, or untracked parts spend that a CMMS like OxMaint can identify and correct within one maintenance cycle.

### How do you calculate fleet maintenance cost per mile?

Divide total maintenance spend (parts + labor + outsourced repairs + tires + fluids + compliance servicing) by total miles driven in the same period. For example, $18,000 in maintenance across 100,000 miles equals $0.18 CPM. The challenge isn't the math — it's capturing every cost line. OxMaint CMMS automatically ties every work order, part issue, and labor entry to the asset and mileage, so your CPM is accurate and real-time. ### What is included in fleet maintenance cost per mile?

Fleet maintenance CPM includes all parts (filters, belts, brakes, tires, fluids, DEF), in-house and outsourced labor, shop supplies, compliance inspections (DOT, emissions, annuals), and minor body repairs. It does not include fuel, insurance, depreciation, or financing — those belong in total operating cost per mile. Downtime cost is sometimes excluded from maintenance CPM but should be tracked separately, as it often exceeds the repair cost itself.
