## The Indiana Portal

[![Indiana state seal](//thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/61/Seal_of_the_State_of_Indiana.svg/250px-Seal_of_the_State_of_Indiana.svg.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Indiana-StateSeal.svg "Indiana state seal")  

[Seal of Indiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seal_of_Indiana "Seal of Indiana")

**[Indiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana "Indiana")** ([/ˌɪndiˈænə/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English "Help:IPA/English") [ⓘ](/wiki/File:En-us-Indiana.ogg "File:En-us-Indiana.ogg") [*IN-dee-AN-ə*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:Pronunciation_respelling_key "Help:Pronunciation respelling key")) is a [state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._state "U.S. state") in the [Midwestern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midwestern_United_States "Midwestern United States") region of the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States "United States"). It borders [Lake Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Michigan "Lake Michigan") to the northwest, [Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michigan "Michigan") to the north and northeast, [Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio "Ohio") to the east, the [Ohio River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_River "Ohio River") and [Kentucky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kentucky "Kentucky") to the south and southeast, and the [Wabash River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wabash_River "Wabash River") and [Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois "Illinois") to the west. Nicknamed "the [Hoosier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoosier "Hoosier") State", Indiana is the [38th-largest by area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_U.S._states_and_territories_by_area "List of U.S. states and territories by area") and the [17th-most populous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_U.S._states_and_territories_by_population "List of U.S. states and territories by population") of the [50 states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_states_and_territories_of_the_United_States "List of states and territories of the United States"). The state's capital and largest city is [Indianapolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indianapolis "Indianapolis"). Indiana was admitted to the Union as the 19th state on December 11, 1816.

[Indigenous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_of_the_Americas "Indigenous peoples of the Americas") resistance to American settlement was broken with their defeat in [Battle of Tippecanoe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Tippecanoe "Battle of Tippecanoe") in 1811, and the collapse of [Tecumseh's confederacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tecumseh's_confederacy "Tecumseh's confederacy") in 1813. The new settlers were primarily Americans of [British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_people "British people") ancestry from the [eastern seaboard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Coast_of_the_United_States "East Coast of the United States") and the [Upland South](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upland_South "Upland South"), and [Germans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germans "Germans"). After the [Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War "American Civil War"), in which the state fought for the [Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_(American_Civil_War) "Union (American Civil War)"), natural gas attracted heavy industry and new [European immigrants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_immigration_to_the_United_States "European immigration to the United States") to its northern counties. In the first half of the 20th century, northern and central sections experienced a boom in goods manufacture and automobile production. Southern Indiana remained largely rural. After the rise and fall of the [Klan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana_Klan "Indiana Klan") in the 1920s, the state swung politically from the [Republican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republican_Party_(United_States) "Republican Party (United States)") to [Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Party_(United_States) "Democratic Party (United States)") in the [New Deal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Deal "New Deal") 1930s. Today, with a decades-long record of returning Republican majorities, Indiana is counted a "[red state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_states_and_blue_states "Red states and blue states")".

Indiana has [a diverse economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Indiana "Economy of Indiana") with a gross state product in 2023 of 404.3 billion. Indianapolis is at the center of the state's largest [metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indianapolis_metropolitan_area "Indianapolis metropolitan area"), with a population of over two million. The [Fort Wayne metro area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Wayne_metropolitan_area,_Indiana "Fort Wayne metropolitan area, Indiana") follows with a population of 645,000. The **[Indiana Statehouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana_Statehouse "Indiana Statehouse")** is the [state capitol building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_state_capitols_in_the_United_States "List of state capitols in the United States") of the [U.S. state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._state "U.S. state") of [Indiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana "Indiana"). It houses the [Indiana General Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana_General_Assembly "Indiana General Assembly"), the office of the [Governor of Indiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_Indiana "Governor of Indiana"), the [Indiana Supreme Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana_Supreme_Court "Indiana Supreme Court"), and other state officials. The Statehouse is located in the capital city of [Indianapolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indianapolis "Indianapolis") at 200 West [Washington Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_Street_(Indianapolis) "Washington Street (Indianapolis)"). Built in 1888, it is the fourth building to house the state government.

The [first statehouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corydon_Historic_District "Corydon Historic District"), located in [Corydon, Indiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corydon,_Indiana "Corydon, Indiana"), is still standing and is maintained as a state historic site. The second building was the old Marion County courthouse which was demolished and replaced in the early 20th century. The third building was a structure modeled on the [Parthenon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parthenon "Parthenon"), but was condemned in 1877 because of structural defects and razed so the current statehouse could be built on its location. (**[Full article...](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana_Statehouse "Indiana Statehouse")**)

## Selected geographic article - [*show another*](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Portal:Indiana&action=purge)

One of the buildings on Main Street in the district

The **[Hartford City Courthouse Square Historic District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hartford_City_Courthouse_Square_Historic_District "Hartford City Courthouse Square Historic District")** is located in [Hartford City, Indiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hartford_City,_Indiana "Hartford City, Indiana"). Hartford City has a population of about 7,000 and is the [county seat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_seat "County seat") of [Blackford County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blackford_County,_Indiana "Blackford County, Indiana") and the site of the county [courthouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courthouse "Courthouse"). The [National Park Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Park_Service "National Park Service") of the [United States Department of the Interior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_the_Interior "United States Department of the Interior") added the Hartford City Courthouse Square Historic District to the [National Register of Historic Places](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Register_of_Historic_Places "National Register of Historic Places") on June 21, 2006—meaning the buildings and objects that [contribute to the continuity of the district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contributing_properties "Contributing properties") are worthy of preservation because of their [historical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History "History") and [architectural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Architectural_style "Architectural style") significance. The District has over 60 resources, including over 40 contributing buildings, over 10 non-contributing buildings, one contributing object (a World War I statue), eight non-contributing objects, and two other buildings that are listed separately in the National Register.

Much of the [District's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historic_district_(United_States) "Historic district (United States)") significance relates to the discovery of [natural gas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_gas "Natural gas") in the [east central](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Central_Indiana "East Central Indiana") region of Indiana. The discovery led to a regional [economic boom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_boom "Economic boom") known as the [Indiana Gas Boom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana_Gas_Boom "Indiana Gas Boom"). Beginning in the late 1880s and lasting for about 15 years, the Gas Boom changed the economy and the appearance of the region. The Hartford City Courthouse Square Historic District is situated in what was the center of Hartford City in the 19th and 20th centuries, and most of the buildings within the District were constructed during the Gas Boom era. The buildings within the District were built in several [architectural styles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Architectural_style "Architectural style"), including Commercial [Italianate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italianate_architecture "Italianate architecture"), [Romanesque Revival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanesque_Revival_architecture "Romanesque Revival architecture"), [Renaissance Revival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance_Revival_architecture "Renaissance Revival architecture"), and others. Many of the buildings' exteriors have not been changed from their original appearance. (**[Full article...](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hartford_City_Courthouse_Square_Historic_District "Hartford City Courthouse Square Historic District")**)

## Selected biography - [*show another*](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Portal:Indiana&action=purge)

Urey in 1934

**[Harold Clayton Urey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harold_Urey "Harold Urey")** [ForMemRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_Member_of_the_Royal_Society "Foreign Member of the Royal Society") ([/ˈjʊəri/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English "Help:IPA/English") [*YOOR-ee*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:Pronunciation_respelling_key "Help:Pronunciation respelling key"); April 29, 1893 – January 5, 1981) was an American [physical chemist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_chemist "Physical chemist") who conducted pioneering work on [isotopes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isotope "Isotope"). He earned the 1934 [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry") "for his discovery of  [heavy hydrogen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deuterium "Deuterium")." He played a significant role in the development of the [atom bomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_weapon "Nuclear weapon"), as well as contributing to theories on the [development of organic life from non-living matter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miller–Urey_experiment "Miller–Urey experiment").

Born in [Walkerton, Indiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walkerton,_Indiana "Walkerton, Indiana"), Urey studied [thermodynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermodynamics "Thermodynamics") under [Gilbert N. Lewis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilbert_N._Lewis "Gilbert N. Lewis") at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley "University of California, Berkeley"). After he received his [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD "PhD") in 1923, he was awarded a fellowship by [the American-Scandinavian Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_American-Scandinavian_Foundation "The American-Scandinavian Foundation") to study at the [Niels Bohr Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niels_Bohr_Institute "Niels Bohr Institute") in [Copenhagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen "Copenhagen"). He was a research associate at [Johns Hopkins University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University "Johns Hopkins University") from 1924 to 1929, before becoming an associate professor of chemistry at [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University "Columbia University"). In 1931, he began work with the separation of isotopes that resulted in the discovery of deuterium. (**[Full article...](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harold_Urey "Harold Urey")**)
