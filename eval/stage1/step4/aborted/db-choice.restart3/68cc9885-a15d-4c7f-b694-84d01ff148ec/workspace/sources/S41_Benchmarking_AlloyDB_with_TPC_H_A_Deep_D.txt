Google Cloud’s AlloyDB for PostgreSQL offers a compelling combination of performance, scalability, and compatibility. This blog post details a benchmark of AlloyDB using the industry-standard TPC-H benchmark, providing insights into its capabilities for analytical workloads. We’ll cover the setup, data loading process, query execution, results analysis, and a concluding summary.

**Setup:**

For this benchmark, we utilized a standard TPC-H 1 GB dataset. The AlloyDB instance was configured as follows:

*   Machine Type: 16 vCPU, 128 GB
*   Storage: 1.37 GB was used in total
*   High Availability: No
*   Version: PostgreSQL 15 compatible
*   Read Pools: No
*   Columnar engine (Enabled): Auto columnarization - Enabled, Memory size -30% (38400 Mb), Set run frequency -EVERY 1 DAYS, Columnar scan -Enabled

**Query Execution:**

The TPC-H benchmark consists of 22 analytical queries designed to test various aspects of a database system’s performance. We executed 20 out of the 22 queries against the loaded dataset. Each query was run twice (cold start and a warm start) to account for potential variations in execution time and obtain more statistically reliable results. We used the `EXPLAIN ANALYZE` command to analyze the query execution plans for potential optimization opportunities. Though Columnar Engine was enabled, we didn’t make use of it.

**Summary:**

The benchmark results demonstrate that AlloyDB exhibits strong performance for TPC-H queries. Queries (Q1, Q3, Q4, Q10, Q13) showcased good aggregation performance whereas Q18 and Q21 would need further investigation. Queries (Q9) showcased good join performance whereas Q18 would need further investigation. Queries (Q2, Q11, Q17) showcased excellent correlated subquery performance.

**Conclusion:**

This benchmark provides valuable insights into AlloyDB’s performance characteristics for analytical workloads. Its scalability and performance capabilities, as demonstrated by the TPC-H results, make it a strong contender for applications requiring high-throughput data analysis. Further investigation could focus on optimizing specific queries identified as potential bottlenecks and exploring the impact of columnar engine on overall performance. This benchmark showcases AlloyDB’s ability to handle demanding analytical workloads effectively, making it a viable option for data warehousing and business intelligence applications. Remember that these results are specific to our chosen configuration; your results may vary depending on your specific setup and workload.
